Now

Updated 16 Nov 2026

Our proposals to partners promise four safeguards: an expert checks the output, the model says when it does not know, the assistant refuses unsafe requests, and the agent asks before it acts. At AIES 2026, papers on each of them described ways they fail that we did not measure yet. I proposed a rule, and in November my team adopted it: when a project promises a safeguard, its evaluation plan says how we will test it.

I also work on our team wiki, which people and AI agents use to find what we know. Agents can now submit sources to it over MCP, and a person reviews each one before it goes in. The evals of its chat assistant now count a refused or cut‑off reply as an error. Before, the judge counted it as a pass.

My research is moving from single answers to AI agents. I study what harm from an AI agent in healthcare looks like and how to detect it, starting with a systematic literature review.

Research

Both of my papers use medical data. One catches wrong answers from small language models. The other corrects a classifier's uncertain predictions with rules a person can read.

Safer On-Premise Medical AI: A Hybrid Framework for Hallucination Detection in Local LLMs

AIES 2026, paper

Hospitals often cannot send patient data to a cloud model, so they run a small model on their own hardware. Our framework flags likely‑wrong answers from medical models of about 1 billion parameters, using sampled answers and the model's hidden states.

  • Paper
  • Code

Interpretable Hybrid Machine Learning Models Using FOLD‑R++ and Answer Set Programming

ICLP 2025, paper

Rules learned with FOLD‑R++ correct a classifier's uncertain predictions and explain them. Accuracy and F1 improved significantly on five medical datasets. With Jesse Heyninck.

  • Code
